# Route map: STOPPED on the development set, 2026-10-05, at $0, before the draw

Pre-registration: `route-map-prereg-2026-10-04.md` (on `main` since `ea2b379`). Milestone 1 and its
development run: `m1-dev-run-2026-10-05.md` (on `main` since `0f63029`). This record answers two
objections the owner's strategy assistant raised against continuing, from the per-case data and the
pre-registration's text, and records the decision. Zero model calls, zero API keys, no extractor
change, no draw. The work folder with the 22 parent trees and their maps stays outside the
repository.

**Decision: the route-map line stops here.** No milestone 1b, no further framework, no freeze, no
draw. The narrow lift of 2026-10-04 is closed, and detection R&D returns to the stop of 2026-10-04
under A1 section 2. No label from section 6 or section 9 applies, because the measurement never ran.

## 1. Objection 1, the ceiling: confirmed, and the binding reading is worse

**The arithmetic as the objection put it is right.** The milestone-1 record counted the in-scope
cases two ways: its counts table puts apostrophe out of scope (14 in scope), its prose puts it in
(15). With 15: 6 resolved, 9 missed. The 9 are the five class-boundary cases (directus, oneuptime,
unleash, backstage, parse-server), signalk, deepstream, apostrophe and erxes. Fixing all five gives
11 of 15, 73%, under 80%.

**One correction lowers it further.** Recomputed from routes whose path is a literal starting with
`/` on a receiver the route-definition rule accepts ("plausible" routes), unleash's map holds
**zero** plausible routes. Unleash declares its routes through a controller convention the
extractor does not read, so class resolution alone cannot reach it. Realistic class-resolution
gain: 4 (directus at 1 import hop, oneuptime at 2, backstage at 3, parse-server whose handlers are
`this.<method>` on the router class). **10 of 15, 67%.**

The other in-scope misses, and whether a static, deterministic change reaches them:

| case | why it misses | static fix | honest? |
|---|---|---|---|
| unleash | routes declared through a controller-class convention | a convention reader for this project | fits one development case |
| apostrophe | routes declared as module-definition object members | a convention reader for this project | fits one development case |
| signalk | the hunk is inside the route-registration function but outside every node | add the enclosing registration function as a node | changes what C2 counts after seeing the result |
| erxes | a GraphQL resolver four import hops from a handler | GraphQL and depth 4 | outside Tier 1 and section 3's depth |
| deepstream | a permission rule on a websocket path, not an HTTP chain | none | not a route |

Without fitting, the ceiling under this reading is **10 of 15 (67%)**. Reaching 12 of 15 (80%)
would take two single-project convention readers written against the development set, which is the
tuning the pre-registration's order of operations exists to prevent.

**But section 3 of the pre-registration defines in-scope differently, and its own reading binds.**
"The framework of a case is read mechanically: the first Tier 1 pattern that matches any defect file
at the parent, else the first Tier 2 pattern, else `unknown`." Applied to the 22 (Express-family
pattern from `route-def-pattern.ts`, plus NestJS, App Router and tRPC tests):

| bar (section 5) | needs | development set now | realistic ceiling after 1b and the remaining Tier 1 frameworks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Tier 1 coverage | ≥ 70% of admitted | **10 of 22, 45%** | **45%**: read from the defect files, so no extractor change moves it |
| 2. C2 in scope | ≥ 80% of in-scope | 6 of 10, 60% | 8 of 10, 80% (parse-server by `this.<method>`, lobe-chat by tRPC) |
| 3. C2 strict | ≥ 60% of all admitted | 6 of 22, 27% | 11 of 22, 50% (13 with the two convention readers, 59%) |

Under the binding text the in-scope bar becomes reachable, but **bar 1 fails as a property of the
case set** and bar 3's ceiling stays under 60%. Under every reading, at least one pre-registered
condition fails at its realistic ceiling, on the development set, which is the most favourable set
this extractor will ever see.

**The finding that matters most: chain-following contributed nothing.** All six C2 hits (budibase,
actual, FUXA, uptime-kuma, parse-dashboard, qinglong) are carried by plausible routes, so the count
stands. But every one is in a defect file that declares routes, and the chain node containing the
hunk is the declaration, the handler or a guard in every case, never a call or data-access node
reached by following. **The map resolved 0 of the 7 cases whose whole fix sits in one non-route
file**, the reach-gap group that the arm-A record named as the map's reason to exist. On the cases
where the map works, the single-file lanes already received the file and missed; on the cases
where the map was needed, it does not reach.

## 2. Objection 2, falsification arm B: mostly an extractor defect, partly arm B's design

**First, a correction to the milestone-1 record.** "More than half the routes carry no guard in 13
of 21 trees" is not C5. C5 also requires an authentication dependency in the root `package.json`.
Computed as written: **5 of 21 trees, and 4 of the 10 in-scope trees (40%)**, so arm B (≥ 30%)
does trip on the development set as written.

**The guard sample.** Two routes per tree with an empty guard chain, drawn by seed over all 21
trees with routes, 42 in all, each read by hand. Categories and the per-sample record are in
`guard-sample-2026-10-05.json` (case identifiers and categories only).

| classification | count |
|---|---|
| **not a route at all** | **29** |
| of which an outbound HTTP client call | 10 |
| of which a store, cache, Map, model or SQL accessor | 15 |
| of which a test mock or harness | 2 |
| of which a non-Express API-spec declaration | 2 |
| **a real route** | **13** |
| of which behind a guard the map missed | 5 |
| of which public by design | 5 |
| of which undetermined | 3 |

**So arm B's trip is, in order:**
1. **An extractor defect: false routes.** In `src/test/lib/route-map.ts`, `routeCall` accepts a
   `get/post/put/delete/all` call on any receiver; `RECEIVER_NAME_RE` is declared and never applied,
   and a non-literal path is accepted on any receiver. Over the 22 maps, **1,402 of 2,872 routes
   (49%) are implausible** by the literal-path-and-receiver rule. On plausible routes only, C5 holds
   in **2 of 10** in-scope trees (20%), and arm B would not trip. Every route count and every
   guard ratio in the milestone-1 record carries this defect; its C1, C2 and C4 results do not
   (section 1).
2. **Missed guards, a real but smaller defect:** 5 of the 13 real routes. Three mechanisms:
   middleware attached to an endpoint-group object; global middleware registered in another file or
   through a security-strategy method; and a global JWT middleware whose link to the routes is lost
   because they are declared on a router passed in as a function parameter.
3. **Arm B's design.** 5 of 13 real routes are public by design. With a public share near 40% on
   this sample, a "more than 50% unguarded" threshold sits close to the natural rate and flags
   legitimate applications. C5 also reads only the root `package.json`: reading every
   `package.json` in the tree raises C5 from 4 to 6 of the 10 in-scope trees, so the auth-dependency
   condition is noisy in the other direction too.

Arm B is fixable in principle: gate route discovery, recognise the three guard mechanisms, and
recalibrate C5. **None of that changes section 1.** Arm B is not what stops this line.

## 3. The three options, decided

- **A, fix and continue: rejected.** The evidence does not show the bar is reachable. Under the
  binding reading, bar 1 fails at 45% from the cases alone and bar 3 tops out at 50 to 59%. Under the
  application-level reading, the in-scope ceiling without fitting is 67%.
- **B, amend before the freeze: rejected as a rescue.** One mis-specification is real and is recorded
  here: section 3 reads a case's framework from its defect files, so every case whose defect sits in
  a service file, the population section 0 and the arm-A record name as the map's purpose, counts
  as "unknown framework" and against coverage by construction, while bar 1's own justification is
  about application frameworks ("the map covers the detector, not the market"). The error was
  detectable from the text before any extractor ran. **But correcting it moves those cases into the
  in-scope denominator, which makes bar 2 harder, not easier**, and the line still fails. Lowering
  80% or 60% would be done because the result disappointed: section 5 gives 80% a reason that does
  not depend on any result. So no amendment is made.
- **C, stop now: chosen.** At $0, before the draw, on the set the extractor was tuned on.

## 4. What the stop means

- **No route-graph work continues.** The extractor, its rehearsal and the fixture stay in
  `src/test/` and `fixtures/route-map/` as tooling, keyless rehearsal in `test:ci`; nothing is
  deleted. The false-route defect in `routeCall` is recorded here and not fixed.
- **No public copy** may say "cross-file", "route graph", "route map" or "application-wide", as
  section 9 requires for a failed line.
- **The narrow lift of 2026-10-04 is closed.** Detection R&D returns to the stop of 2026-10-04 under
  A1 section 2 with everything frozen there.
- **The three access-control lanes keep their measured result** (Arm A 0 of 12 on the shipped
  pipeline). The owner's product decision on them, keep, drop or rebuild as deterministic checks, is
  taken on that record. This record adds one fact to it: on the 22 development cases, the defects
  the map can contain are the ones whose file the lanes already received, so the binding limit on
  those cases is the judgment applied to a file already in hand, not reach.
- The measurement population was never drawn, so no case was spent. The third held-out set stays
  sealed and untouched.
